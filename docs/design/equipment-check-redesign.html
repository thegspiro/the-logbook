Three ways to rebuild the Equipment Check page

The tab is a personal to-do list wearing a fleet-readiness badge. Below: what it gets wrong today, then three rebuilds — from a one-week reordering to a new deficiency lifecycle — each drawn as the screen it would actually be.

Where it stands

What the page does today

Equipment Checks renders one component for everybody: a grid of cards, one per (shift × template) assignment, sorted by date. Under it, a collapsed accordion of your own completed checks. Under that, a row of tiles linking out to Templates, Patterns, Reports, Check Reports, Supply, Settings and Platoons.

Current state. Six cards of identical weight; two are five days overdue, two are two days away, and nothing on the page says which matters.

01 Everyone gets the member's page

The officer wants to know whether the rig is in service and whether the crew did their checks. The inventory manager wants to know what is broken, expired or short across the fleet. Both get “My Equipment Checklists” — a personal to-do list — plus a link farm.

02 The apparatus is not an object on this page

Everything is organised by checklist assignment. Nobody in a firehouse thinks that way. They think “is E-1 good?” — and the page cannot answer it, because a truck's state is scattered across six cards, another page, and a report.

03 A failure goes in and never comes out

Finding the broken thing is the entire point of a check. A failed item is written into the check record and then only visible in the failure log on the admin-only Check Reports page. Nothing tracks it from found to fixed, and nothing tells the crew running that rig tomorrow.

04 Due today looks exactly like due next Tuesday

Sorting is correct — soonest first — but every card is the same size, colour and weight, so a five-day-overdue check and a check that has not opened yet compete equally for attention.

05 One domain, five destinations

Equipment Checks, Apparatus Inventory, Supply & Expiring, Check Reports, and Templates under Settings. They share data and a mental model; they share no navigation, and the seam shows as a grid of tiles at the bottom of the page.

06 Nothing summarises before it lists

There is no count, no rate, no status band. You cannot tell whether this page needs you without reading all of it — which on a phone, at shift change, is the moment it is least likely to get read.

The direction

B  +  Check log

Fleet Board, with the seven squares opened up into a page

Option B unchanged: the apparatus is the unit, the fleet board is the front door. The seven-day strip on each rig card stops being decoration and becomes the summary of a real sub-page — the last N checks across the whole fleet, so “are things okay?” is a question you can answer by looking rather than by trusting.

Where it sits

One component, two scopes. At /scheduling/equipment/checks it covers the fleet and is reached from the board header or by tapping any rig's strip. Scoped to one rig it is the Check log tab of the apparatus detail — same filters, same rows, apparatus filter pinned. It replaces today's “Completed checklists” accordion, which only ever showed you your own checks.

The check log at fleet scope. Grid answers “is the pattern okay?” — E-2's block of amber reads at a glance, and so does T-3's single missed morning on the 5th. Log answers “what happened on that one?”

Four decisions worth making explicitly

01 A missed check has to be a row

Today nothing is written when a check does not happen — the absence is only visible as a card that never left “Not started”. The grid needs the negative, so the log is built from expected checks (shift × active template) left-joined onto submitted ones, not from the check records alone. This is the one genuinely new query.

02 “Last X” means duty days, not calendar days

A rig that runs a weekly check would show six empty columns in a seven-day window and look neglected. Columns are check occasions for that apparatus, so B-7's weekly check gives it four squares in a fortnight and a 100% rate — correct, and legible next to E-1's twenty-eight.

03 Out of service is not the same as missed

E-2 was OOS from the 11th. Marking those days as missed punishes the crew for a rig in the shop and makes the rate meaningless. Out-of-service days render as not-scheduled and are excluded from the denominator, with the reason available on the row.

04 Who sees whose checks

The log names members, so it is not the current my-checks-only accordion with a wider query. Fleet scope sits behind equipment_check.view; a member with only equipment_check.submit gets the same component filtered to their own checks, which is strictly more than they have today.

Member
Their own log, finally filterable by rig and date instead of a flat list searchable by name.
Officer
The assurance view. Two weeks of the fleet in one screen, and the pattern — not the incident — is what shows.
Inventory manager
Filter to failures across the window to see which items keep coming back. Exports as CSV for the same reason the reports page does.
Added cost over plain B
Small. One expected-vs-actual endpoint with a window parameter, one route, one component used at two scopes.
What it retires
The “Completed checklists” accordion, and the compliance tab of Check Reports becomes a thin wrapper over the same data.

What is being built

B, plus the check log — the apparatus is the object all three people are actually thinking about, and the log turns the one number an officer most wants (“has this been getting done?”) from a report they have to go and pull into a page they can glance at. A and C are not on the plan; the parts of them worth keeping fold in.

  1. Fleet board and apparatus detail. The new front door at /scheduling/equipment, with per-rig detail absorbing /scheduling/apparatus-inventory and /scheduling/supply/expiring as tabs. Settle the in-service rule with the department before writing it — the pill is a claim the app is making.
  2. Check log, both scopes. Fleet-wide at /scheduling/equipment/checks, and the same component as the apparatus detail's Check log tab. Needs the expected-vs-actual query so missed checks exist as rows.
  3. Borrowed from A: the urgency band and the collapsed future, applied to the member's strip at the top of the fleet board. Cheap, and it fixes the “due today looks like due Tuesday” problem without needing A's page.
  4. Borrowed from C: nothing yet, deliberately. If the deficiency lifecycle ever gets built, the fleet board is already the right surface for it — the per-rig counts on each card become links into a worklist instead of into a filtered log.

The one question C answers that this does not is who skipped their check, as opposed to which rig went unchecked. If that turns out to matter, it is a column on the check log rather than a new lens — the log already names the member on every row.
