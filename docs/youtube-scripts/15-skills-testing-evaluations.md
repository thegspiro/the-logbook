# Script 15: Skills Testing — Running an Evaluation the Member Can Actually See

**Video Type:** Focused Tutorial (Medium-Form)
**Estimated Length:** 18–22 minutes
**Target Audience:** Training Officers, evaluators, FTOs/preceptors, EMS officers
**Roles Covered:** training_officer, safety_officer, examiner
**Chapters:** 8 (each designed as a standalone clip)
**Requires permission:** `training.manage` for official tests — **practice tests
need no permission at all**

> **Companion videos:** Script 5 is the broad Training Officer guide and mentions
> skills testing in passing. This is the deep dive. Script 11 builds the pipeline
> a skills test can complete; Script 10 is the member's side of that pipeline.

> **What changed on 2026-08-08:** most of chapters 4 through 7 is new. If you have
> the older skills-testing footage, chapters 1–3 still hold; the rest does not.

## CHAPTER 2: Building a Skill Sheet (1:30 – 5:00)

### THE TEMPLATE (1:30 – 2:30)

**[SCREEN: Training Admin > Skills Testing > Templates. Click New Template.]**

> "A template is a blank skill sheet. Give it a name, pick a category, set the
> passing percentage, and set a time limit if the skill has one."

**[SCREEN: Fill in metadata for "Patient Assessment — Trauma", passing 70%,
time limit 10 minutes.]**

> "Then sections, and criteria inside them. A section is a phase of the skill —
> Scene Size-Up, Primary Survey. A criterion is one thing you're watching for."

### CRITICAL CRITERIA (2:30 – 3:45)

**[SCREEN: Add three criteria; check Required on one of them.]**

> "Check **Required** on the ones that are automatic failures. That's the
> critical-criteria box at the bottom of an NREMT sheet — miss one and it doesn't
> matter what the score says, it's a fail."

**[CALLOUT: "Required = automatic fail, regardless of score"]**

> "Now watch the form when I check that box."

**[SCREEN: Check Required; the Passing Points field appears beneath it.]**

> "**Passing Points only shows up on critical criteria.** That's new, and it's
> worth a second, because the old behavior confused people. It used to render on
> every scored criterion with a little '(critical only)' note next to it — so the
> form was asking you for a number that the scorer then ignored, because a
> non-critical criterion contributes points and can't fail the test on its own."

**[SCREEN: Uncheck Required; the field disappears. Re-check it; the previous
value is still there.]**

> "And notice: unchecking Required **keeps** the number. It doesn't wipe it. That
> looks untidy, and it's deliberate — an empty threshold falls back to zero, so
> if you toggled that box off and back on by accident, a cleared field would leave
> the criterion quietly passing at any score. Better inert than wrong."

### PUBLISH (3:45 – 5:00)

**[SCREEN: Click Publish; confirm.]**

> "Publish it. Only published templates can be used for a test."

> "One important thing about editing a published template, and I'm going to come
> back to this later with a real example: **editing it from now on only affects
> future tests.** Every test freezes the sheet it was created against. That was
> not true before August of 2026, and it caused a genuinely nasty class of
> problem."

## CHAPTER 3: Running an Evaluation (5:00 – 8:30)

### STARTING A TEST (5:00 – 5:45)

**[SCREEN: Tests tab > New Test. Pick template, pick candidate, leave Practice
Mode off.]**

> "Pick the sheet, pick the candidate, and go. Leave Practice Mode off for an
> official evaluation — we'll come back to practice, because practice works
> differently now."

**[SCREEN: If the candidate has used their attempts, the refusal appears.]**

> "If the requirement this sheet is linked to caps attempts, and the candidate's
> used them, **you get told right here** — before you take them out to the bay,
> not after you've run a twenty-minute evaluation that can't count. That check
> runs again on submit, because you can start several tests before submitting
> any."

**[CALLOUT: "Attempts checked before AND after — you can't run a test that can't
count"]**

### SCORING ON A PHONE (5:45 – 7:15)

**[B-ROLL: Phone in one hand, propped against a rail, examiner watching a
candidate in turnouts.]**

**[SCREEN: The active test screen on a 390px viewport. Tap criteria pass/fail.]**

> "This screen is meant to be used one-handed, outdoors, while you're actually
> watching somebody. Tap through the criteria. The running score is at the top."

> "It **saves itself.** That's new. It used to save only when you pressed Save or
> moved into review — which is fine right up until your phone locks, or the tab
> gets killed to free memory, and everything you scored since the last time you
> thought about saving is gone."

**[CALLOUT: "Autosave — while the test is live"]**

> "Save still works. You just don't have to remember it anymore."

### THE TIMER (7:15 – 8:30)

**[SCREEN: The timer at the top of the active test.]**

> "Time limits are pass/fail criteria on most sheets, so the clock matters."

> "The number on that stopwatch is what gets recorded. Also new — and the old
> behavior was bad enough to be worth naming. It used to throw your reading away
> on submit and record 'finished minus started' instead. The start is stamped
> once, when the test first goes in progress. So a test you began at nine in the
> morning and submitted after lunch recorded **seven hours**."

**[CALLOUT: "The stopwatch is the record. Wall clock is only a fallback."]**

**[SCREEN: Navigate away and back; the timer resumes at its previous value.]**

> "And if you close it and come back, the timer picks up where it was instead of
> restarting at zero."

## CHAPTER 4: Two People, One Test (8:30 – 9:45)

**[SCREEN: Split screen — a phone with unsaved criteria, and a desktop with the
same test open in the officer's records tab.]**

> "Here's a situation that used to lose work quietly. Two examiners on one
> evaluation. Or — more common — you're scoring on your phone in the bay, and a
> training officer opens the same test on the desktop to fix a typo."

**[SCREEN: The desktop saves. The phone attempts a save; a conflict notice
appears and the autosave indicator goes to "paused".]**

> "One side used to overwrite the other, and **the side that lost got a success
> message.** That's the part that mattered — you had no way to know."

> "Now the out-of-date save is refused, autosave stops, and it tells you. Reload,
> and re-enter whatever you scored in the meantime."

**[CALLOUT: "Autosave pauses on conflict — it won't pretend to be saving"]**

> "It deliberately does not keep retrying. A save that can't succeed retried every
> thirty seconds, silently, while you go on scoring, is worse than stopping."

## CHAPTER 5: Who Sees the Result (9:45 – 14:00)

### THE DECISION (9:45 – 10:45)

**[B-ROLL: A training file open on a desk, handwritten notes in the margin.]**

> "This chapter isn't really about a setting. It's about a decision your
> department has to make, and the software used to make it for you."

> "Before August 2026, completing a test showed the candidate the whole scorecard
> immediately — every mark, every point, and **every note you wrote.**"

> "That's exactly right for a Tuesday-night drill. It is exactly wrong for a
> promotional evaluation. And your notes are usually working notes for the
> training file — 'hesitant, needed two prompts' — not feedback you drafted for
> the member to read."

### THREE QUESTIONS (10:45 – 12:30)

**[SCREEN: Training Admin > Configuration > the Skills-Test Results group.]**

> "So it's three separate questions now. **What** they see, **when** they see it,
> and **who else** can."

**[SCREEN: The "What the member sees" control, cycling through the three
options.]**

> "**What.** Full results — everything, and that's the default, so nothing
> changed for you unless you change it. Scores only — every mark and every point,
> and all the written commentary stripped out. Or nothing at all."

**[CALLOUT: "Full · Scores only · Nothing"]**

**[SCREEN: The "When" control.]**

> "**When.** On completion, which is the default. Or on release — the finished
> result stays invisible until an officer releases it. That's for the evaluation
> you want a chief to read first, or the failure you'd rather deliver in person
> than by notification."

**[SCREEN: Set What to "Nothing"; the When control disappears.]**

> "Set it to nothing and the 'when' question goes away, because there's nothing
> left to time."

**[SCREEN: A template's own settings, overriding the department default.]**

> "Set the department norm here, then override it on a single skill — or on a
> single test — where the norm doesn't fit. A promotional sheet can be
> 'scores only, on release' while everything else stays open."

### WHO ELSE (12:30 – 13:15)

**[SCREEN: A test's Viewers panel; add a preceptor.]**

> "**Who else.** Name people on a specific test — a preceptor, an FTO. Or list
> corporate positions whose holders can read results generally."

**[CALLOUT: "A viewer never sees more than the candidate does"]**

> "One rule that's worth stating out loud: **a named viewer never sees more of a
> result than the candidate does.** If the department shows the candidate scores
> only, the preceptor gets scores only. There's no version of 'sharing a result'
> where the observer sees more of it than the person it's about."

### RELEASING ONE (13:15 – 14:00)

**[SCREEN: Records tab; a completed unreleased result with a Release action.]**

> "To release one, it's right here in the records tab, next to Void."

> "You'll notice Release shows on any unreleased result, not just ones under the
> 'on release' mode. That's on purpose — the action is safe to press twice and it
> refuses tests whose results are never shown anyway, so you don't have to work
> out which mode a given sheet inherited before you can act."

**[TRANSITION: Withdrawing]**

## CHAPTER 6: When a Result Shouldn't Stand (14:00 – 16:30)

### THREE DIFFERENT THINGS (14:00 – 15:15)

**[SCREEN: The records tab; hover three different rows showing three different
actions.]**

> "Three things can go wrong with a test, and they're three different actions.
> The records tab only ever offers you the right one for that row."

**[CALLOUT: "Delete (practice) · Void (scored) · Cancel (unscored)"]**

> "**Delete** is for a practice attempt. Gone, no trace, fine."

> "**Void** is for a completed official result that shouldn't stand — wrong
> candidate, wrong sheet, an evaluation that was compromised. It **keeps** the
> record, with a reason you have to type and your name on it. It comes out of your
> pass rate and your average score. And if that pass had completed a training
> requirement, **the requirement is released** — it goes back to incomplete."

**[SCREEN: Click Void; the reason dialog; type a reason; confirm. Show the
requirement returning to incomplete on the member's pipeline.]**

> "You can't delete an official result anymore. A scored evaluation is a record of
> what an examiner observed. You withdraw it. You don't erase it."

> "**Cancel** is for an evaluation you abandoned mid-session — the candidate got
> called out, the pump failed, whatever. Partial scoring is kept and the test is
> closed out. No reason required, and nothing to release, because nothing was
> credited."

### PRACTICE (15:15 – 16:30)

**[SCREEN: New Test with Practice Mode on. Note: no permission gate.]**

> "Practice changed more than anything else here. It used to be a dead end for
> the person taking it — the result lived on your device, because every
> skills-testing screen required officer permission."

**[CALLOUT: "Any member can run a practice test on a peer"]**

> "Now **any member** can run a practice test on a peer. Two probies can drill
> each other on a Saturday without an officer standing there. Official tests still
> need `training.manage`."

> "The candidate can see their own practice results, and delete them. They're
> their notes. They're kept for a year and then they're gone. They never count
> against an attempt limit, they never touch compliance, and they're never held
> back for release — there's nothing for the department to release, because it
> isn't the department's record."

**[SCREEN: An officers-only template; attempt to start a practice test as a
regular member; it isn't offered.]**

> "One edge: a template marked officers-only stays officers-only. You can't see a
> restricted sheet by starting a practice test against it."

**[TRANSITION: The member's phone]**

**[SCREEN: Log in as the candidate. Training > My Training. Scroll to the

> "Here's the other side. My Training now has a **Skills Tests** section — their

**[SCREEN: Open a result; show the frozen scorecard, the pass/fail, the section

> "That's it. That's the whole feature from their end. It sounds small. It
> replaces 'can I see that?' followed by you turning your phone around."

**[SCREEN: A voided result showing its reason to the member.]**

> "If you voided a result, they see the reason. Not just that it's gone."

**[SCREEN: The empty state on an account with a withheld result.]**

> "And if your department withholds results, a withheld one simply isn't in the
> list. Not greyed out, not 'you don't have permission' — absent."

**[CALLOUT: "Withheld reads as absent, not as forbidden"]**

> "That's deliberate and it's worth understanding. A 'you may not view this'
> message announces _'you were evaluated and you may not know how it went'_, which
> is worse than silence for everybody. So the empty state says results the
> department doesn't share won't appear here — without claiming one is being
> withheld, which usually it isn't."

## CHAPTER 8: The Bug Worth Knowing About (18:30 – 21:00)

### WHAT USED TO HAPPEN (18:30 – 20:00)

**[SCREEN: A completed scorecard from March. Then the template editor, inserting
a criterion into the middle of a section. Then the same scorecard, marks visibly
shifted.]**

> "I said I'd come back to this. Before August 2026, editing a published template
> **changed scorecards that were already finished.**"

> "Here's why. Criteria are identified by where they sit on the sheet — section
> two, criterion four. And editing a template rewrote the one stored copy of that
> structure. Completed tests read from it."

**[CALLOUT: "Insert a criterion → recorded marks shift onto their neighbours"]**

> "So inserting a criterion in the middle **shifted every recorded mark below it
> onto the wrong line.** Deleting one dropped its recorded result off the
> scorecard. And raising the passing percentage could turn a recorded pass into a
> fail — retroactively, on an evaluation that happened months ago."

> "None of that announced itself. The scorecard just quietly said something
> different than it had."

### WHAT HAPPENS NOW (20:00 – 21:00)

**[SCREEN: Edit a published template again; the old scorecard is unchanged.]**

> "Every test now carries a snapshot of the sheet it was created against —
> structure and scoring rules both. Editing a template affects future tests and
> nothing else."

> "One thing to be straight about, if you're watching this and worrying about your
> historical records: tests completed **before** this shipped were backfilled from
> the current template. The structure they were originally scored against was
> overwritten in place and can't be recovered. The current template is already
> what those tests were displaying, so nothing you look at changed — what the
> backfill bought is that they're now frozen against _future_ edits."

**[CALLOUT: "If you edited a published sheet before Aug 2026, spot-check old
scorecards"]**

> "If you edited a published sheet before August 2026 and you have evaluations
> that matter — promotional, certification — it's worth spot-checking a few
> against whatever paper or memory you have. Going forward it can't happen."

### OUTRO (21:00 – 21:30)

> "That's skills testing. Build the sheet once. Score it one-handed in the bay.
> Decide deliberately how much the candidate sees. And when something has to come
> off the books, withdraw it with a reason instead of erasing it."

**[CALLOUT: "The Logbook — docs/training/09-skills-testing.md"]**

## Production Notes

- Chapter 8 is the one to lead with if you're cutting a single clip for existing
  users — it describes a data-integrity problem they may have lived through.
- Chapter 5 needs two accounts on screen (officer and candidate) to land. Record
  the member's side on a phone.
- Shorts 15F and 15G both describe **historical** data that was not
  retroactively corrected. Do not cut the caveat.
- The 390px captures in Chapter 3 should be real device footage, not a browser
  resize — the point is that it is usable outdoors one-handed.
