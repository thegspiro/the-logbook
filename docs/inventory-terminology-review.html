One word, three systems: how “inventory” is used

The department already tracks gear, apparatus stock and medical supplies — in three separate places that share one word and one permission. Everything is trackable today; almost nothing is separable.

Date 16 Aug 2026 Branch claude/inventory-terminology-review-wwti84 Findings 10 (1 fixed, 2 high)

Section 01

What “inventory” currently means

Four subsystems hold stock. They were built at different times, and the vocabulary never got reconciled — so the same word covers a shirt in a closet, a bag of gauze in a compartment, and a halligan bolted to a rig.

Nothing here is redundant — these are genuinely different jobs. The problem is that only the third has a name a firefighter would recognize, and the first two are both called “inventory” or “equipment” depending on which screen you are standing on.

Section 02

Medical supplies already work. They are just never called that.

The shelf-to-truck loop is real and it is good. Lot numbers, expiration dates, first-expiring-first-out consumption, restock flags raised by the crew who used the last one, and a weekly alert that separates “swap it” from “order it”. That is a medical supply system by any standard.

It has no name, no item type, and no mention in any document. The word “medical supplies” appears exactly once in the codebase: in a code comment explaining what ItemType.EQUIPMENT is for.

01 · SHELF Inventory lot A batch of 4×4 gauze with its own lot number, expiration and on-hand count.
→
02 · LINK Checklist position A compartment slot that names the catalog item and how many belong there.
→
03 · TRUCK Deployed lots Each lot physically aboard, so a slot holding three lots reports the soonest date.
→
04 · ALERT Weekly notice Expiring on the truck, split by whether in-date stock is behind it.

So the answer to “can we track all of these things” is yes, and better than most departments manage on paper. The answer to “can we separate them into their categories” is where it falls down.

Section 03

Findings

F-1

Every stock alert has been silently undelivered

Fixed on this branch

Low-stock, NFPA-retirement and expiring-supply alerts each picked recipients with u.role in ("admin", "owner", "quartermaster"). User has no scalar role column — roles are the many-to-many positions relationship — so every comparison raised AttributeError. The per-org guard caught it, logged it, and moved on: the task reported success and nobody was ever emailed.

The tests passed because they built recipients from MagicMock, which answers .role with a mock. Recipients now resolve through the inventory.manage permission via the roles relationship, and the tests build users from roles carrying real permissions.

backend/app/services/scheduled_tasks.py · three call sites, now one helper

F-2

One permission pair governs uniforms, PPE, medical stock and the store

High

inventory.manage is all-or-nothing across the whole catalog. There is no way to give an EMS supply officer the medical stock while the quartermaster keeps the uniforms — the moment you grant one, you have granted both, plus write-offs, charges and member issuance records for everything.

This is the single blocker for the flexibility you described. Every other gap below is downstream of it.

backend/app/core/permissions.py · all 20 inventory routes gate on this one string

F-3

The Apparatus Officer role ships with full catalog and store control

High

The seeded apparatus_officer role — described as “day-to-day fleet tracking, maintenance logging, and equipment checks” — carries inventory.manage and storefront.manage. A department that appoints one gives them the power to rewrite the uniform catalog, approve write-offs, and reprice the department store.

That is a consequence of F-2, not a deliberate grant: the checklist work they legitimately need lives behind equipment_check.manage, and the catalog read they need is inventory.view.

F-4

No medical item type — supplies land in equipment or consumable by habit

Medium

ItemType offers uniform, ppe, tool, equipment, vehicle, electronics, consumable, other. Medical supplies fit two of those equally badly, and which one a department picks decides which fields the form shows: consumable gets quantity and unit only, equipment gets serial and asset tag. Neither surfaces expiration, even though the lot system tracks it.

F-5

“Equipment” means five different things

Medium

My Equipment is a member’s issued gear. An Equipment Request asks the quartermaster for some. An Equipment Kit is a bundle template. Apparatus Equipment is what is bolted to a rig. An Equipment Check is the morning walk-around. A member reading the nav has no way to tell which screen holds their turnout coat.

Nav labels, route paths, model names and one permission family

F-6

Two links to the catalog, only one of them enforced

Medium

CheckTemplateItem.inventory_item_id is a real foreign key with ondelete="SET NULL". ApparatusEquipment.inventory_item_id is a bare String(36) with an index and no constraint — delete the catalog item and the rig keeps pointing at a row that is gone.

F-7

ItemType.VEHICLE duplicates the apparatus module

Low

A department can file an engine as an inventory item with a serial number and an asset tag, entirely outside the fleet module that exists to track it. Nothing reconciles the two.

F-8

“Medical” is already taken by the screening module

Low

medical_screening handles member physicals and fit tests — protected health information, with its own permissions and cache exclusions. Naming a supply domain “medical” without qualification risks a reader, or a future route, conflating a firefighter’s medical record with a box of gauze.

F-9

The store lives inside the inventory permission category

Low

storefront.* is filed under PermissionCategory.INVENTORY, so the role editor presents selling to members and controlling department stock as one subject. They are different jobs with different money attached.

F-10

Only one stock office can be named

Low

OFFICE_CATALOG has a Quartermaster entry and nothing else in the supply line. A department that splits the job cannot name the second holder, so correspondence about medical stock signs out as the Quartermaster regardless of who actually handles it.

Section 04

Recommendation

Keep one catalog. Name the domains inside it, and make stewardship of a domain grantable on its own. A department with a single quartermaster sees no change; a department that splits the role gets to.

The steps are ordered because each depends on the one before it. Steps 1–3 are the substance; 4 and 5 are cleanup that gets cheaper the longer it is not deferred.

1

Give medical supplies a name of their own

Add MEDICAL to ItemType — EMS Supplies in the UI, to stay clear of the screening module — and give it the field set the domain actually needs: quantity, unit, lot and expiration. Both the category form and the items filter read this list already, so one enum addition surfaces in both.

Then stop the convention where equipment quietly means medical, and say so in the model docstring that currently carries it.

2

Split the manage permission by domain, keeping the wildcard

Add inventory.manage_uniform, inventory.manage_medical and inventory.manage_ppe alongside the existing inventory.manage. The permission matcher already treats inventory.* as covering every inventory.<action>, so a department running everything through one quartermaster grants the wildcard and nothing changes for them.

Endpoints that touch a specific category check the domain-scoped permission with inventory.manage as an accepted alternative — require_permission() is already OR-logic, so this is additive and backward compatible.

3

Let a category declare who stewards it

Add a nullable steward_position_id to inventory_categories, pointing at a position. Alerts, equipment requests, reorder requests and return reviews for that category route to the holder; categories that leave it null fall back to whoever holds inventory.manage, which is the behaviour restored in F-1.

The precedent already exists: IssuanceAllowance scopes a limit by category and position, so category-plus-role is an established shape in this schema rather than a new idea.

4

Correct the seeded roles and add a supply office

Strip inventory.manage and storefront.manage from apparatus_officer, leaving inventory.view and the equipment_check.* set the role is described as needing. Add an ems_supply_officer system role holding inventory.manage_medical plus equipment_check.manage — the two halves of the shelf-to-truck loop — and a matching OFFICE_CATALOG entry so its holder can sign correspondence.

Existing organizations keep whatever their roles were customized to; only the seeded defaults change.

5

Retire the ambiguous labels, and put the FK back

In member-facing copy, “equipment” should mean one thing. My Equipment reads better as My Issued Gear; Equipment Request as Gear Request; the apparatus walk-around stays Equipment Check, which is what crews call it. Route paths and table names can stay — this is a copy change, not a migration.

Separately, give ApparatusEquipment.inventory_item_id the same real foreign key its checklist counterpart has, and decide whether ItemType.VEHICLE should survive alongside the fleet module.

Section 05

What already does what you asked for

Worth stating plainly, because the findings above are all gaps and the picture would otherwise read worse than it is:

  • Uniform gear and medical supplies already live in different physical spots. StorageArea nests rack → shelf → box inside a room, and every item points at one. The separation you described physically is already modelled.
  • Categories nest. parent_category_id means a domain can be a top-level category with children under it without any schema change.
  • Per-category limits are already role-aware. Issuance allowances are keyed on category plus position, which is the exact shape step 3 extends.
  • Both tracking modes exist. Serial-numbered one-to-one assignment for a turnout coat, quantity pools for gauze — with lots and expiry layered on the pool side.

The tracking is built. What is missing is the vocabulary that tells a department which pile they are looking at, and the permission granularity that lets two people own two piles.
